Country Name: Türkiye
Short Description: Bridging two continents, Türkiye offers incredible variety, from deep-blue coves and ancient cities to lively metropolises and fairy-tale valleys. On a single trip you can swim in the sea, explore thousands of years of history and chase one regional flavour after another.
Country Overview
Türkiye sits where Europe and Asia meet, and it carries the traces of many civilisations, which makes it a remarkably rich travel destination. From the historic streets of Istanbul to the otherworldly valleys of Cappadocia, and from Antalya's Mediterranean coast to the lush green highlands of the Black Sea, every region has an atmosphere of its own.
One of the best things about the country is that you can build completely different routes depending on how you like to travel. A beach holiday, a food trip, a history and culture route, hiking in nature or a lively city break: in Türkiye you can do each of these separately or combine them all in one journey.
Most Popular Cities
Istanbul: With its Bosphorus views, historic peninsula, palaces, bazaars and vibrant city life, it is one of the most visited cities in Türkiye. Sultanahmet, Galata, Karaköy, Kadıköy and the Bosphorus shore are ideal for discovering the city's different faces.
Antalya: One of the key addresses for a Mediterranean holiday. Thanks to the historic streets of Kaleiçi, its ancient cities, waterfalls and long beaches, it is great not only for summer holidays but also for cultural trips.
Izmir: It combines the relaxed Aegean atmosphere with city life. After watching the sunset on the Kordon promenade, you can easily reach popular spots such as Alaçatı, Çeşme, Urla, Foça and Ephesus.
Muğla: The province that is home to some of Türkiye's best-loved holiday spots, including Bodrum, Marmaris, Fethiye, Datça and Akyaka. It stands out especially in summer with its coves, boat trips and natural beauty.
Nevşehir: Located at the heart of Cappadocia. With Göreme, Ürgüp, Uçhisar, underground cities and hot-air balloons, it offers one of the most unusual travel experiences in Türkiye.
Trabzon: A good starting point for anyone who wants to explore the nature of the Black Sea region. Trips to Sümela Monastery, Uzungöl and the surrounding highlands are very popular.
Gaziantep: A reason to travel in its own right, especially for food lovers. The Coppersmiths' Bazaar, the Zeugma Mosaic Museum and its rich cuisine are among the city's greatest strengths.
Places to Visit
The Bosphorus: The symbol of Istanbul, bringing the European and Asian shores together in a single view. Crossing it by ferry or taking a Bosphorus cruise is one of the most enjoyable ways to see the city.
Cappadocia: One of the most distinctive regions of Türkiye, with its fairy chimneys, valleys, rock formations and hot-air balloons. Spending a few days around Göreme, Uçhisar and Ürgüp is ideal.
Pamukkale: Its white travertine terraces and the ancient city of Hierapolis right above them bring nature and history together in one place.
Ephesus: One of the most important cities of the ancient world. The Library of Celsus and the Great Theatre are among the most impressive structures on the site.
Ölüdeniz: The symbol of Fethiye, with its turquoise sea and the mountain scenery around it. Paragliding from Mount Babadağ is one of the area's most famous activities.
Mount Nemrut: One of the most extraordinary historic sites in Türkiye, with its colossal statues and, above all, its sunrise and sunset views.
Lake Salda: A natural area that stands out for its pale shores and turquoise tones.
Mardin: With its stone architecture, narrow streets and views stretching over the Mesopotamian plain, it offers an experience quite different from the classic holiday routes.
Sultanahmet and Hagia Sophia: With Hagia Sophia, the Blue Mosque, the Basilica Cistern and Topkapı Palace nearby, Istanbul's historic peninsula is one of the densest history areas you can explore on foot.
Kaş and Kalkan: Turquoise bays, diving spots and quiet streets make these small towns favourites on the Lycian coast.
Historic Sites
Türkiye's historic heritage is not limited to a handful of cities. Across Anatolia there are thousands of buildings and archaeological sites from different periods.
Göbeklitepe: An important archaeological site near Şanlıurfa, dating back to the Neolithic period.
Ephesus: One of the most impressive ancient cities in Anatolia, with structures from the Roman period.
Troy: An archaeological site in Çanakkale with thousands of years of history across its many settlement layers.
Topkapı Palace: The palace complex in Istanbul that served for many years as one of the administrative centres of the Ottoman Empire.
Ani: A historic city near Kars, very impressive with its churches, city walls and wide steppe landscape.
Sümela Monastery: A historic monastery built on steep cliffs in the Maçka district of Trabzon.
Aspendos Theatre: A Roman-era theatre in Antalya that has survived to the present day in very good condition.
Hierapolis: The ancient city right above Pamukkale has a theatre, a necropolis and an ancient pool.
Süleymaniye Mosque: One of Mimar Sinan's greatest works in Istanbul, ideal for seeing Ottoman architecture up close.
Nature
Thanks to Türkiye's geographic diversity, a journey of just a few hours can take you to completely different landscapes.
Kaçkar Mountains: A favourite of nature lovers, with highland pastures, misty valleys, lakes and hiking trails.
Lycian Way: A long-distance hiking trail running between Fethiye and Antalya. It combines Mediterranean views with historic ruins.
Butterfly Valley: One of the striking coves near Fethiye, surrounded by steep cliffs.
Mount Ida (Kaz Dağları): A good choice for those who want a quiet holiday in nature, with its forests, villages and walking trails.
Yedigöller: A popular spot for photography and nature trips, especially in autumn when the forests change colour.
Munzur Valley: Home to some of the most impressive landscapes of Eastern Anatolia, with mountains, rivers and vast natural areas.
Köprülü Canyon: A deep, green canyon near Antalya known for rafting and hiking.
Abant Lake: A calm lake among the forests of Bolu, especially beautiful in its autumn colours.
Uzungöl: A lake in Trabzon surrounded by mountains and forests, close to the Black Sea highlands.
Local Food
Food culture in Türkiye changes considerably from region to region. So when you travel, it is worth looking beyond the few dishes everyone knows.
Kebab: There are many different varieties, especially in and around Gaziantep, Adana and Şanlıurfa.
Mantı: A traditional dish of small dumplings filled with minced meat, usually served with yoghurt and sauce.
Lahmacun: A thin dough topped with a minced meat mixture and baked in a stone oven.
Pide: Depending on the region, it can be prepared with minced meat, cheese, diced meat and other toppings.
Olive oil dishes: A staple of Aegean and Mediterranean cuisine in particular. Artichokes, stuffed vine leaves and a range of vegetable dishes stand out.
Baklava: One of Türkiye's most famous desserts, made with thin layers of pastry, pistachios or walnuts, and syrup.
Künefe: A warm dessert made with shredded pastry, cheese and syrup.
Simit: A sesame-covered bread ring, popular at breakfast or as a quick snack.
Turkish coffee: Traditional coffee served in small cups.
Menemen: A breakfast favourite made with tomatoes, peppers and eggs.
İskender: Bursa's famous dish of thinly sliced döner served over pide with butter and tomato sauce.
Budget and Price Level
Overall level: Mid-range.
Travel costs in Türkiye vary widely depending on the city, the season and the type of holiday. In Istanbul, Bodrum, Çeşme and some popular coastal areas, accommodation and restaurant prices can rise sharply, especially in the summer season.
In smaller cities, away from the main seaside resorts, and in local businesses, it is possible to find more affordable options.
In big cities with good public transport, travelling without renting a car can reduce your budget considerably. Intercity buses are also a practical way to get around Türkiye cheaply.
When to Go
Türkiye can be visited in all four seasons, but the ideal time depends entirely on your route. Spring and autumn are the most comfortable periods for exploring most of the country.
March – May: Very pleasant for Istanbul, the Aegean, Cappadocia and cultural trips. The weather is usually milder and the summer crowds have not yet arrived.
June – August: The busiest period for beach holidays in Antalya, Muğla, Izmir and other coastal areas. Temperatures can be very high in July and August.
September – October: One of the most enjoyable times of the year to combine a beach holiday with a cultural trip. The sea can still be warm, especially on the Aegean and Mediterranean coasts.
December – February: The ski season takes centre stage in places such as Uludağ, Erciyes, Palandöken and Kartalkaya. Cappadocia's snowy landscapes are also very impressive at this time.
⭐ Favourite period for a general trip: April–May and September–October.
Visa Information
Citizens of the Republic of Türkiye naturally do not need a visa or passport for travel within the country.
For foreign visitors, visa requirements vary by nationality and passport type. Citizens of some countries can enter Türkiye without a visa, while other visitors may need an electronic or a standard visa.
As visa and entry rules can change over time, it is recommended to check the current conditions with the official sources of the Republic of Türkiye before travelling.
Getting Around
As Türkiye is a large country, the best way to travel depends on your route.
Plane: There are domestic flights between Istanbul, Ankara, Izmir, Antalya, Gaziantep, Trabzon, Diyarbakır and many other cities. Flying saves time over long distances.
Bus: The intercity bus network is very extensive. It is also useful for reaching small towns and districts.
Train: High-speed train lines such as Ankara–Istanbul and Ankara–Konya, along with other routes, offer a comfortable alternative between some cities.
Metro & tram: Big cities such as Istanbul, Ankara, Izmir and Bursa have a well-developed public transport network.
Car rental: Very useful, especially on the Aegean and Mediterranean coasts, for those who want to explore coves, villages and small towns.
Ferry: One of the most enjoyable ways to get around Istanbul day to day. There are also sea connections between some coastal cities and islands.
Where to Stay
Where you stay in Türkiye depends a great deal on the kind of holiday you want. In big cities, central areas with good transport links are an advantage, while on coastal routes you may choose based on closeness to the sea, quieter coves or proximity to nightlife.
Istanbul – Sultanahmet & Sirkeci: Very suitable for first-time visitors who want to explore the historic sights on foot. Close to key places such as Hagia Sophia, the Blue Mosque, Topkapı Palace and the Grand Bazaar.
Istanbul – Galata, Karaköy & Beyoğlu: A good choice for those who love cafés, restaurants, historic streets and a lively city atmosphere. Both the historic and the modern side of the city are within easy reach.
Istanbul – Kadıköy & Moda: Ideal for those who want a more local Istanbul experience. With their cafés, restaurants, seafront and busy streets, these are areas especially loved by young travellers.
Antalya – Kaleiçi: Offers a romantic and lively atmosphere with historic houses, small boutique hotels and restaurants around the harbour.
Antalya – Lara & Konyaaltı: Better suited to those who want to focus on a beach holiday. There are many hotels and other places to stay close to the beaches.
Muğla – Bodrum: Stands out for those looking for luxury hotels, beach clubs, restaurants and nightlife. For a quieter holiday, consider Yalıkavak, Gümüşlük and the surrounding coves.
Muğla – Fethiye & Ölüdeniz: A good base for holidays centred on the sea, nature and activities. Boat trips, coves and nature routes are all easy to reach.
Muğla – Datça & Akyaka: Appeals to those who want to get away from the big resorts and spend a calmer holiday close to nature.
Cappadocia – Göreme: One of the most practical options for first-time visitors to the region. Being close to restaurants, tour departure points and the valleys is a big advantage.
Cappadocia – Uçhisar: Very good for those who want a quieter, scenic, boutique stay. Hotels with valley views and cave-style rooms stand out in particular.
Izmir – Alsancak: Suitable for those who want to stay in the city centre, close to the Kordon and the restaurants.
Izmir – Alaçatı & Çeşme: A good choice for a beach holiday, boutique hotels, restaurants and lively summer evenings.
Black Sea – around Rize & Trabzon: Those who want to be close to nature can consider highland houses, bungalows and small mountain hotels instead of the city centres.
💡 A small tip: There is no single "best area to stay" in Türkiye. Choosing by transport links in Istanbul, closeness to the sea on the Aegean and Mediterranean coasts, and the view in Cappadocia makes the holiday much more enjoyable.
Getting There
As Türkiye is a major aviation hub between Europe, Asia and the Middle East, it can be reached by direct flights from many cities around the world.
Istanbul Airport (IST) and Sabiha Gökçen Airport (SAW) are among the main hubs for international flights.
Antalya, Izmir, Dalaman and Bodrum-Milas airports are especially useful for visitors heading to the tourist regions.
There are also road connections from neighbouring countries. Depending on your route, you can consider buses, a private car or, on some routes, trains.
Travel Tips
* Currency: Turkish lira (₺ / TRY)
* Debit and credit cards are widely accepted in big cities, but carrying some cash can be useful for small businesses and some local markets.
* When exploring Istanbul, choosing the metro, Marmaray, tram and ferry over a car at rush hour can save you time.
* The Aegean and Mediterranean coasts can be very hot in the summer months; it is more comfortable to visit historic sites early in the morning.
* If you are planning a balloon flight in Cappadocia, it is a good idea not to leave the booking to the last day.
* Because Türkiye is so large, it is more enjoyable to plan regional routes than to squeeze distant places such as Istanbul, Cappadocia and Antalya into a programme of just a few days.
* Don't stick only to the popular restaurants in tourist areas; local tradesmen's restaurants (esnaf lokantası) are one of the best ways to discover the cuisine of different cities.
* When visiting mosques and other religious buildings, it is important to dress appropriately and to be mindful of prayer times.
* Plug type: C and F.
* Emergency number: 112.
* Especially in the summer season, booking accommodation in advance in popular areas such as Bodrum, Çeşme, Antalya, Kaş and Fethiye gives you more options.
* Tea is offered almost everywhere; accepting a glass in a local business is a nice way to start a conversation.
* Gentle bargaining is normal in bazaars and markets, but prices in supermarkets and restaurants are fixed.

Country Name: United States
Short Description: With its world-famous metropolises, vast national parks, long coastal roads, deserts, tropical regions and states that differ completely from one another, the United States is far too large and varied to explore in a single trip. From the skyscrapers of New York to the beaches of California, from the Grand Canyon to Yellowstone, and from Miami's beaches to Hawaii's volcanic islands, every region is a travel route in its own right.
Country Overview
The United States is a vast country in North America made up of 50 states. It shares land borders with Canada and Mexico and has coastlines on both the Atlantic and the Pacific Ocean.
The capital is Washington, D.C., but cities such as New York, Los Angeles, Chicago, San Francisco, Miami, Las Vegas and Boston are among the places that stand out most for tourism.
Because of the country's size, there are big differences between regions in climate, culture, architecture and natural surroundings. Northeastern cities such as New York and Boston offer an intense urban and historical experience, while the California coast, Florida's tropical atmosphere and the desert landscapes of the Southwest make for completely different trips.
With its national parks, the USA is also one of the world's most important nature destinations. Places such as the Grand Canyon, Yellowstone, Yosemite, Zion and the Great Smoky Mountains are highlights for discovering the country's natural diversity.
Most Popular Cities
* New York City: One of the most visited cities in the country, with its skyscrapers, museums, Broadway shows, Central Park and diverse neighbourhoods.
* Los Angeles: One of California's major tourism centres, with Hollywood, Pacific beaches, the film industry and its sprawling layout.
* San Francisco: Known for the Golden Gate Bridge, its hilly streets, historic cable cars and bay views.
* Las Vegas: Popular for its huge hotels, shows, entertainment venues and the desert routes around it.
* Miami: One of Florida's most important destinations, with its beaches, Art Deco architecture, tropical climate and Latin American-influenced culture.
* Chicago: One of the country's major cities, with its skyscrapers, architecture, museums and the shore of Lake Michigan.
* Washington, D.C.: The political and historic centre of the country, with the White House, the Capitol, monuments and major museums.
Places to Visit
* Times Square: One of New York's best-known spots, with its giant screens, theatres and lively atmosphere.
* Central Park: The large city park in the heart of Manhattan.
* Statue of Liberty: One of the country's most recognised symbols, in New York Harbor.
* Empire State Building: One of New York's classic skyscrapers and a major viewpoint.
* Brooklyn Bridge: The historic bridge connecting Manhattan and Brooklyn.
* Golden Gate Bridge: One of the most iconic structures of San Francisco and California.
* Alcatraz Island: The historic prison island in San Francisco Bay.
* Hollywood Walk of Fame: One of Los Angeles's popular spots linked to film and entertainment culture.
* Griffith Observatory: One of the best places to take in the view of Los Angeles and the Hollywood Sign.
* National Mall: The wide area in Washington, D.C. that holds the Capitol, the Washington Monument and various memorials.
Historic Sites
* Independence Hall: An important building in Philadelphia linked to the Declaration of Independence and the founding of the country.
* Liberty Bell: One of the key symbols of American independence, in Philadelphia.
* Freedom Trail: A historic walking route in Boston connecting important sites of American history.
* Lincoln Memorial: One of the country's best-known monuments, built in Washington, D.C. in memory of Abraham Lincoln.
* United States Capitol: The landmark building in Washington, D.C. that houses the US Congress.
* White House: The official workplace and residence of US presidents.
* Mount Vernon: George Washington's historic home and estate in Virginia.
* Colonial Williamsburg: A large historic area in Virginia that reflects town life in the colonial period.
* Gettysburg National Military Park: The historic site of one of the most important battles of the American Civil War.
Nature
* Grand Canyon National Park: The giant canyon in Arizona carved by the Colorado River and one of the country's most iconic natural areas.
* Yellowstone National Park: One of the world's best-known national parks, with geysers, hot springs, wide valleys and wildlife.
* Yosemite National Park: One of California's most impressive natural areas, with giant granite cliffs, waterfalls and valleys.
* Zion National Park: Popular for its towering canyon walls and hiking trails in Utah.
* Bryce Canyon National Park: One of Utah's major parks, with unusual rock spires and orange-red scenery.
* Arches National Park: Notable for its natural stone arches and desert landscapes.
* Death Valley National Park: One of California's characteristic natural areas, with desert scenery and unusual geological formations.
* Great Smoky Mountains National Park: One of the country's most popular national parks, with forests, mountains and misty valleys.
* Rocky Mountain National Park: Stands out for its high mountains, lakes and hiking trails in Colorado.
Local Food
* Hamburger: A classic American dish prepared in countless ways in different parts of the country.
* Hot dog: A popular street food with different local versions, especially in cities such as New York and Chicago.
* New York pizza: One of New York's characteristic foods, with its large, thin slices.
* Chicago deep-dish pizza: A style of pizza unique to Chicago, with a thick crust and generous fillings.
* Barbecue: Texas, Kansas City, the Carolinas and other regions each have their own ways of cooking and saucing it.
* Buffalo wings: Chicken wings prepared with a spicy sauce.
* Mac and cheese: A classic American dish made with pasta and cheese sauce.
* Lobster roll: A seafood sandwich that is especially popular in New England.
* Clam chowder: A thick seafood soup associated with New England.
* Gumbo: One of the characteristic dishes of Louisiana and New Orleans cuisine.
* Jambalaya: A Louisiana dish made with rice, meat or seafood, and spices.
Budget and Price Level
Overall level: High
Travel budgets in the United States vary greatly by state and city. Costs can be very high in places such as New York, San Francisco, Los Angeles, Miami and Hawaii.
* Accommodation: Hotel prices are high in big city centres. Taxes and additional resort fees can increase the total price.
* Food: There is a very wide price range, from fast food to high-end restaurants.
* Tipping: Tipping is common in restaurants and many service sectors. Keep in mind that the menu price may not fully reflect what you finally pay.
* Taxes: Sales taxes vary by state and area and may be added on top of the price on the label.
* Car rental: One of the major budget items, especially in the western USA and on national park routes.
* Domestic flights: They save time over long distances, but extras such as baggage may be charged separately.
* National parks: If you plan to visit several national parks, it can be worth comparing the pass options.
* New York: One of the most expensive destinations for accommodation and restaurants.
* Hawaii: Can require a high budget because of flight, accommodation, food and car rental costs.
When to Go
* March – May: A lovely time for New York, Washington, D.C., California and many other cities.
* June – August: Popular for most national parks, but tourist numbers are high.
* September – October: One of the best periods for city trips, national parks and road trips.
* October – November: Popular for seeing the autumn colours in New England.
* December: A busy period for the Christmas atmosphere in New York and other big cities.
* December – March: Ski season in Colorado, Utah and other mountain regions.
* Florida: Winter and spring are among the periods when temperatures are more comfortable.
* Southwestern deserts: Spring and autumn are more suitable for avoiding the extreme summer heat.
* Alaska: June to August is the most popular period for nature and long daylight hours.
* Hawaii: Can be visited all year round, but regional rainfall and ocean conditions change with the season.
Because the country is so large, the best time to travel should be chosen according to the region you plan to visit.
Visa Information
Holders of ordinary (burgundy) passports of the Republic of Türkiye need an appropriate US visa to travel to the United States as tourists. One of the most common categories for tourist visits is the B1/B2 visitor visa.
The USA is not part of the Schengen system. A visa for the Schengen area, the United Kingdom or any other country does not give the right to enter the USA.
Holding a valid US visa does not automatically guarantee entry to the country either. The final decision on entry is made by the authorities at border control.
As visa application procedures, fees, interview conditions and entry rules can change over time, it is important to check the latest information from official US visa and consular sources before travelling.
Getting Around
* Domestic flights: Because of the country's size, one of the most practical ways to travel between distant cities.
* Subway: Available in big cities such as New York, Washington, D.C., Chicago, Boston and San Francisco.
* Bus: Common for getting around cities and on some intercity routes.
* Train: Useful in the Northeast Corridor, especially between Boston, New York, Philadelphia and Washington, D.C.
* Long-distance trains: They can offer scenic routes between states, but take much longer than flying.
* Car rental: One of the most practical options for exploring California, Florida, the national parks and routes in the western USA.
* Taxis and ride-hailing apps: Widely available in big cities.
* Bicycle: Can be used in some cities and around national parks.
* Walking: Certain parts of New York, Boston, Washington, D.C. and San Francisco are well suited to exploring on foot.
Where to Stay
* New York – Midtown Manhattan: Very central for reaching Times Square, Central Park and the main tourist sights.
* New York – Lower Manhattan: Practical for those who want to be close to the Financial District, the Brooklyn Bridge and various neighbourhoods.
* New York – Brooklyn: Offers a range of options for those who want a more local atmosphere with easy access to Manhattan.
* Los Angeles – Hollywood: Popular with first-time visitors and those who want to be near the city's classic tourist spots.
* Los Angeles – Santa Monica: Great for trips centred on the beach and coastal life.
* San Francisco – Union Square: Practical for central transport links and shopping.
* San Francisco – Fisherman's Wharf: Popular with those who want to be near the bay and the tourist sights.
* Miami – South Beach: One of the most popular choices for the beach, the Art Deco district and lively city life.
* Miami – Brickell: Suits those who want a modern city atmosphere, restaurants and high-rise hotels.
* Las Vegas – The Strip: The most practical area for being close to the big hotels, shows and entertainment venues.
* Chicago – The Loop: A central location close to the city's architectural and cultural sights.
* Washington, D.C. – Downtown: Practical for reaching the National Mall, museums and monuments.
* Boston – Back Bay: Popular for the historic centre, shopping and transport.
* Honolulu – Waikiki: One of Hawaii's most popular places to stay, close to the beach, restaurants and tourist services.
Getting There
The United States can be reached from Türkiye by direct and connecting flights from Istanbul. Flight options to New York, Los Angeles, Miami, Chicago, Washington, D.C., San Francisco and other major cities can vary depending on the travel period.
For New York, John F. Kennedy International Airport and Newark Liberty International Airport are among the main gateways. Los Angeles International Airport, Miami International Airport and San Francisco International Airport are the key airports for their cities.
Because of the size of the USA, it is more practical to focus on one region than to try to see the whole country in a single trip.
For a first trip, you could plan an East Coast route such as New York – Washington, D.C. – Boston.
For the West Coast, a San Francisco – Los Angeles – San Diego route is a good choice. On longer trips, you can add nature and road-trip routes such as Yosemite or California Highway 1.
For trips focused on national parks, the Las Vegas – Grand Canyon – Zion – Bryce Canyon route is very popular.
In Florida, Miami – Florida Keys – Everglades – Orlando can be planned as a separate route.
Travel Tips
* The currency is the US dollar (USD).
* Because the country is so large, it is important not to compare the distances between cities with those on trips in Europe.
* Focusing on one region gives you a more relaxed and affordable route than trying to see many states in a single trip.
* Baggage rules on domestic flights vary by airline, so it is important to check the total cost when buying tickets.
* Tipping is very common in restaurants. What is expected can vary by type of service.
* Sales tax may be added at the till to the prices shown in shops, so the amount you pay can be higher than the figure on the label.
* Renting a car is not necessary for most tourist trips to New York; the subway and walking can be more practical.
* In sprawling cities such as Los Angeles a car can be more convenient, but traffic and parking costs should be taken into account.
* On national park trips, seasonal reservations may be required for some popular roads, campsites or entry systems.
* In big national parks such as the Grand Canyon, it is important to allow time not only for the viewpoints but also for hikes and travel times.
* Summer temperatures can reach dangerous levels in Death Valley and other desert areas.
* In mountainous national parks, snow and sudden weather changes can occur at high altitudes even in summer.
* In places such as Yellowstone, you must not approach wild animals and must strictly follow park rules.

Country Name: Canada
Short Description: With the majestic Rocky Mountains, turquoise glacial lakes, vast forests, modern cities and impressive nature stretching from the Atlantic to the Pacific, Canada is one of the richest outdoor destinations in the world. From Toronto's modern city life to Vancouver's ocean and mountain views, and from Banff's turquoise lakes to the historic streets of Québec City, every part of the country offers a different travel experience.
Country Overview
Canada lies in the north of North America and is one of the largest countries in the world by area. It has coastlines on the Atlantic, Pacific and Arctic Oceans and shares a long land border with the United States to the south.
The capital is Ottawa, but Toronto, Vancouver, Montréal and Québec City are among the cities that stand out most for tourism.
In the west, the provinces of British Columbia and Alberta are at the heart of nature travel, with the Rocky Mountains, glacial lakes and national parks. Banff and Jasper are among Canada's best-known national parks.
In the east, the province of Québec offers a completely different atmosphere with its French-influenced culture and historic cities, while Atlantic Canada stands out for its rocky coasts, small fishing towns and wild nature.
Because Canada is so large, focusing on one region makes for a more relaxed itinerary than trying to see the whole country in a single trip.
Most Popular Cities
* Toronto: One of Canada's largest cities. It stands out for the CN Tower, its museums, multicultural neighbourhoods and the shore of Lake Ontario.
* Vancouver: A city set between the Pacific Ocean and the mountains, offering nature and city life side by side.
* Montréal: One of Canada's most distinctive cities, with French-influenced architecture, gastronomy, festivals and a historic centre.
* Québec City: One of the country's most beautiful cities, with a walled historic centre and architecture reminiscent of European towns.
* Ottawa: The capital of Canada. Known for its parliament buildings, museums and the Rideau Canal.
* Calgary: One of Alberta's big cities and a key starting point for Banff and Rocky Mountain routes.
* Victoria: The capital of British Columbia, on Vancouver Island; popular for its historic buildings, harbour and gardens.
Places to Visit
* CN Tower: One of the best-known modern structures in Toronto and in Canada.
* Toronto Islands: The islands opposite downtown Toronto, popular for their parks and city views.
* Niagara Falls: One of the world's best-known waterfall systems, on the border between Canada and the USA.
* Stanley Park: Vancouver's large park, bringing together coastline, forest and city views.
* Granville Island: Popular in Vancouver for its markets, restaurants and art spaces.
* Capilano Suspension Bridge: The famous suspension bridge spanning a forested valley near Vancouver.
* Old Montréal: The most characteristic part of Montréal, with cobbled streets and historic buildings.
* Notre-Dame Basilica of Montréal: One of the city's best-known historic buildings, with a richly detailed interior.
* Old Québec: The city centre on the UNESCO World Heritage List, with its walls, cobbled streets and historic buildings.
* Château Frontenac: One of the most recognisable buildings on the Québec City skyline.
Historic Sites
* Old Québec: One of the best-preserved walled historic districts in North America.
* Citadelle of Québec: An important part of Québec City's historic defence system.
* Château Frontenac: The grand building that has become part of Québec City's historic identity.
* Old Montréal: The district of historic streets and buildings dating back to the city's founding years.
* Notre-Dame Basilica: One of Montréal's important religious and historic buildings.
* Parliament Hill: Buildings at the centre of Canada's political history.
* Rideau Canal: A UNESCO World Heritage Site running between Ottawa and Kingston.
* Fort Henry: A historic fort in Kingston that reflects 19th-century Canadian military history.
* L'Anse aux Meadows: A UNESCO World Heritage Site in Newfoundland with the archaeological remains of a Viking settlement.
Nature
* Banff National Park: One of Canada's most famous national parks, with the Rocky Mountains, glacial lakes, forests and wildlife.
* Lake Louise: One of Banff's most iconic natural spots, with turquoise water and mountain views.

For first-time visitors to Egypt, the Cairo – Giza – Luxor – Aswan route is one of the classic itineraries covering the country's most important ancient monuments. On this route, Giza, Saqqara, Luxor, Philae and Abu Simbel give you the chance to see different periods of Ancient Egypt.
Between Luxor and Aswan you can travel by train, by road or by boat on the Nile.
Those who want to combine history with a beach holiday can continue to Hurghada or Sharm El-Sheikh after visiting Cairo and Luxor.
On longer trips, Alexandria, Siwa Oasis, Dahab or Abu Simbel can be added to the route.
Travel Tips
* The currency is the Egyptian pound (EGP).
* Although cards can be used in tourist areas, it is useful to carry cash for small businesses and local markets.
* Visiting the Pyramids of Giza early in the morning can be more comfortable in terms of both heat and crowds.
* The historic sites in Luxor are very extensive. The visit is more enjoyable if you do not squeeze Karnak, the Valley of the Kings and the other major sights into a single day.
* The entry arrangements for the tombs in the Valley of the Kings can differ; it is important to check during your visit which tombs are included in the standard ticket.
* Hot-air balloon flights at sunrise are among the popular activities in Luxor.
* Abu Simbel is a long way from Aswan, so travel times need to be planned in advance.
* A felucca trip on the Nile is a lovely experience, especially in Aswan.
* Summer temperatures in the south of Egypt can be very high. It is important to carry water, sunscreen and a hat.
* When visiting religious buildings, local dress codes should be respected.
* Bargaining is common in bazaars and markets.
* For camel or horse rides, guiding and other services offered in tourist areas, it is useful to agree the price before the service begins.
* It may be better to choose reliable bottled drinking water rather than tap water.

Country Name: United Arab Emirates
Short Description: With futuristic skyscrapers, luxurious city life, vast deserts, impressive mosques and beaches on the shores of the Persian (Arabian) Gulf, the United Arab Emirates brings together a modern city experience and Arab culture. From Dubai's world-famous landmarks to Abu Dhabi's cultural centres, and from desert safaris to the mountain scenery of Hatta, shopping, entertainment, culture, nature and a beach holiday can all be combined in one trip.
Country Overview
The United Arab Emirates lies in the east of the Arabian Peninsula, on the shores of the Persian (Arabian) Gulf and the Gulf of Oman, and is made up of seven emirates: Abu Dhabi, Dubai, Sharjah, Ajman, Umm Al Quwain, Ras Al Khaimah and Fujairah.
The capital is Abu Dhabi. With the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque, Louvre Abu Dhabi, a modern city centre and a long coastline, it is one of the major centres of cultural and city tourism.
Dubai is the country's best-known city for international tourism, with the Burj Khalifa, Palm Jumeirah, Dubai Marina and giant shopping malls. Alongside the modern districts, you can see the city's traditional past around Al Fahidi and Dubai Creek.
Sharjah offers different travel options with its museums and cultural venues, Ras Al Khaimah with its mountains and outdoor activities, and Fujairah with its Gulf of Oman coast and dive sites.
Most Popular Cities
* Dubai: The country's most popular city, with skyscrapers, beaches, shopping malls, luxury hotels and entertainment options.
* Abu Dhabi: The capital of the United Arab Emirates. It stands out for its grand mosques, museums, coastline and modern architecture.
* Sharjah: One of the country's major centres of art and culture, with museums, traditional markets and cultural buildings.
* Ras Al Khaimah: Offers an experience different from the cities, with the Hajar Mountains, beaches and outdoor activities.
* Fujairah: Popular for its beaches on the Gulf of Oman, mountain scenery and dive sites.
* Al Ain: A city in the Emirate of Abu Dhabi known for its oases, historic buildings and the mountain Jebel Hafeet.
* Ajman: An alternative to Dubai and Sharjah, with quieter beaches and a small-city feel.
Places to Visit
* Burj Khalifa: The world's tallest skyscraper, in Dubai, and the city's most important landmark.
* Dubai Mall: One of the largest shopping malls in the world, with shops, restaurants and entertainment areas.
* Museum of the Future: One of Dubai's modern landmarks, with futuristic architecture and technology-themed exhibitions.
* Palm Jumeirah: The artificial island designed in the shape of a palm tree and one of Dubai's best-known projects.
* Dubai Marina: Popular for its marina surrounded by skyscrapers, its restaurants and long promenades.
* Burj Al Arab: One of Dubai's iconic buildings, with its sail-shaped architecture.
* Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood: Important for seeing the atmosphere of old Dubai, with traditional buildings and narrow lanes.
* Dubai Creek: The waterway that divides the city's historic trading centre in two, known for its traditional abra boats.
* Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque: One of the most impressive religious buildings in Abu Dhabi and in the country.
* Louvre Abu Dhabi: An important museum that brings together art, history and works from different civilisations.
Historic Sites
* Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood: An important historic district reflecting Dubai's traditional architecture from before the modern skyscrapers.
* Al Fahidi Fort: One of Dubai's old defensive structures.
* Dubai Creek: A historic area linked to the city's pearl trade and maritime past.
* Al Shindagha Historical District: Important for discovering Dubai's traditional life and maritime past.
* Al Jahili Fort: One of the country's major historic forts, in Al Ain.
* Al Ain Oasis: A UNESCO World Heritage Site with its traditional falaj irrigation system and palm groves.
* Qasr Al Hosn: One of the oldest stone buildings in Abu Dhabi and an important historic centre that tells the story of the city's development.
* Hili Archaeological Park: Important for its archaeological remains, thousands of years old, near Al Ain.
* Fujairah Fort: One of the best-known historic buildings in Fujairah.
Nature
* Arabian Desert: One of the country's most characteristic natural regions, with great sand dunes and desert landscapes.
* Liwa Oasis: Notable for the giant dunes and desert scenery in the south of the Emirate of Abu Dhabi.
* Empty Quarter / Rub' al Khali: The part of one of the world's largest continuous sand deserts that extends into the United Arab Emirates.
* Hajar Mountains: The mountains in the east of the country offer an experience completely different from the city skylines, with valleys and outdoor activities.
* Jebel Jais: The country's highest mountain, in Ras Al Khaimah, and a major centre for adventure tourism.
* Hatta: One of the popular nature trips from Dubai, with mountains, a reservoir and outdoor activities.
* Hatta Dam: Popular for kayaking and scenic outings, with turquoise water and the surrounding mountains.
* Jebel Hafeet: A mountain near Al Ain and a viewpoint with wide desert panoramas.
* Al Ain Oasis: A green oasis surrounded by thousands of date palms.
Local Food
* Al machboos: One of the best-known dishes of Emirati cuisine, made with spiced rice and meat or fish.
* Al harees: A traditional dish made by cooking wheat and meat for a long time.
* Thareed: A hearty traditional dish made with bread, vegetables and meat.
* Madrouba: A thick dish made with rice, spices and various proteins.
* Balaleet: A traditional breakfast dish made with sweet vermicelli, spices and egg.
* Regag: A thin, crisp traditional Emirati bread.
* Chebab: A traditional pancake that can be flavoured with cardamom and saffron.
* Samak mashwi: Grilled fish prepared with spices.
* Saloona: A spiced traditional stew made with meat or chicken and vegetables.
* Luqaimat: A popular dessert of small fried dough balls served with date syrup or sweet syrup.
* Khameer: A lightly sweet and spiced traditional bread.
Budget and Price Level
Overall level: Mid-range – High
The United Arab Emirates is known for its luxury travel options, especially in Dubai and Abu Dhabi, but there are also hotels, restaurants and transport options to suit different budgets.
* Accommodation: Luxury hotels can be very expensive. In Dubai, more affordable alternatives can be found in areas such as Deira, Bur Dubai and Al Barsha.
* Food: There is a very wide choice, from luxury restaurants to inexpensive Indian, Pakistani, Lebanese and other Middle Eastern cuisines.
* Transport: The Dubai Metro is economical and convenient. Taxi fares are higher than public transport but practical for reaching many destinations.
* Activities: The Burj Khalifa, theme parks, desert safaris and private beach clubs can raise the total budget considerably.
* Shopping: International brands are common in the big malls. Traditional souks offer a range of price levels.
* High season: Accommodation prices can rise in the period from November to March.
* Summer: Because it is very hot, some hotels may offer lower prices.
When to Go
* November – March: The most comfortable period for city sightseeing, desert activities and outdoor events.
* December – February: The period when daytime temperatures are very pleasant and tourist numbers rise.
* March – April: The weather is hot but can still be suitable for outdoor activities.
* May: Temperatures begin to rise noticeably.
* June – September: The weather can be very hot and humid. It can be hard to spend long periods on outdoor activities.
* October: A transitional period when temperatures start to fall.
For exploring Dubai, Abu Dhabi and desert routes together, November to March is one of the most comfortable periods.
Visa Information
For citizens of the Republic of Türkiye, entry requirements for the United Arab Emirates can vary by passport type.
Holders of ordinary (burgundy) passports need to check the United Arab Emirates visa rules in force on their travel date for tourist trips. Where applicable, visa applications can be made through airlines, authorised tourism companies or the relevant official channels.
The arrangements for special, service and diplomatic passports may differ from those for the burgundy passport.
As visa exemptions, lengths of stay and application procedures can change, it is important to check the current requirements with the official immigration authorities of the United Arab Emirates and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of the Republic of Türkiye before travelling.

* Taxi: Common throughout the country and easy to find in tourist areas.

* Bicycle: An option on certain seafront and cycle paths in Dubai and Abu Dhabi.
* Walking: Areas such as Downtown Dubai, Dubai Marina and the Abu Dhabi Corniche are suitable for walking, but because of the long distances across the cities you need to combine it with public transport.

Getting There
The easiest way to reach the United Arab Emirates from Türkiye is by plane. There are many direct flights from Istanbul to Dubai and Abu Dhabi. Depending on the travel period and airline schedules, there may also be direct flights to Sharjah.
Dubai International Airport is one of the country's main international gateways, and thanks to its metro connection it is very easy to reach the city centre.
Abu Dhabi Zayed International Airport is practical for routes to Abu Dhabi and Yas Island.
It is easy to travel by road between Dubai and Abu Dhabi. The two cities can comfortably be combined in one trip.
For first-time visitors, the Dubai – Abu Dhabi route is a good start for seeing the country's modern cities and important cultural buildings.
On longer trips, you can add Hatta, Ras Al Khaimah, Al Ain or Fujairah and explore the desert, mountain and coastal regions as well.
Travel Tips
* The currency is the United Arab Emirates dirham (AED).
* Card and contactless payments are very common, especially in Dubai and Abu Dhabi.
* Because Dubai is a very spread-out city, when choosing a hotel it is important to look at how close it is to a metro station and not only to the centre.
* Downtown Dubai and Dubai Marina are a long way apart. Dividing your daily routes by area saves time.
* It is useful to buy tickets in advance for popular sights such as the Burj Khalifa in high season.
* Visits to the Burj Khalifa around sunset can be especially busy.
* For desert safaris, it is important to check the tour content, type of vehicle, camp programme and included services before booking.
* When visiting the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque, the dress code must be followed.
* At religious and cultural sites, it is important to respect local rules and photography restrictions.
* During Ramadan, opening hours, events and the rhythm of daily life may be different.
* Because temperatures are very high in the summer months, it is more comfortable to plan outdoor activities for the early morning or the evening.
* Air conditioning indoors can be very strong, so it may be useful to carry a light extra layer.
* Although dress codes are more relaxed on beaches and in resorts, it is important to dress in a way that respects local culture in public spaces in the city.
